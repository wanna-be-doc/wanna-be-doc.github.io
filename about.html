About

A student's curiosity, turned into a mission.

High school student and author, writing for the next generation of curious minds.

Biography

Meet Sanjana

Sanjana Krishnan is a high school student from California with a passion for medicine, neuroscience, and neurobiology. Her writing focuses on educational nonfiction for children, with an emphasis on explaining careers and real-world topics in a clear and accessible way.

Outside of writing, she spends time researching scientific topics, reading about healthcare, and learning how scientific research can be applied to improve lives.

🧬

What Fascinates Her

Human biology and neuroscience, with a particular fascination with neuroplasticity — the brain's remarkable ability to rewire itself in response to injury, learning, and experience. She is captivated by how the same organ that stores a childhood memory can rebuild pathways lost to a stroke.

🔬

What She's Exploring

The effects of music-based therapies on post-stroke rehabilitation — how rhythm and melody can help rewire neural pathways and restore motor function in recovery patients.

💡

Real-World Impact

Fascinated by how advances in technology are transforming patient care, from AI-assisted diagnostics to wearable health monitors. She believes bridging the gap between innovation and clinical practice is key to better health outcomes.

Why She Wrote the Book

From an idea to a global initiative

Most medical content available to young readers is either oversimplified or buried in dense terminology that makes the field feel out of reach. Sanjana wrote So You Want to Be a Doctor to bridge that gap — making medicine accessible, engaging, and less intimidating for middle schoolers without sacrificing substance.

The book uses clear explanations, illustrated diagrams, real-world case scenarios, and diagnostic thinking exercises to give young readers a genuine sense of how doctors approach the human body. Each chapter is designed to build curiosity, not just knowledge.

What started as a single book quickly evolved into a global health literacy initiative. Through partnerships with Rotary International, Books for Africa, Book Aid International Swindon (UK), and WAAW Foundation, Dallas, copies have been distributed to schools, communities, and children's hospitals across eight countries — including India, the United States, Ghana, Nigeria, Rwanda, Mexico, the United Kingdom, and Malaysia — reaching hundreds of students who might not otherwise have access to science and medical education resources.

The initiative continues to grow, with new school partnerships, community distributions, and hospital placements expanding the book's reach into more classrooms and waiting rooms around the world.
